# Plan: Zuordnungs-Kreuzwort

## The puzzle
- A filled **17x8** grid with every block shown and no clue numbers.
- **Pattern:** about as many blocks as the original (22 of 136 cells), every white cell part of
  an entry of at least 2 letters, all white cells connected, no entry longer than **10 letters**.
- **Hints in the grid:**
  - One randomly chosen entry of 5 or more letters, written in full.
  - Every occurrence of one letter, picked at random from the grid's 4 most frequent letters.
  - The two hints may overlap.
- **Clues:** shuffled and split into *Waagerecht* and *Senkrecht*, with **no letter counts** and
  no numbers. The solver has to work out which clue belongs to which slot.
- **Words:**
  - Umlauts are written AE/OE/UE, at `--min-score 50`.
  - Allowed: proper names, abbreviations, plurals, infinitives, adjective base forms,
    lexicalized participles, and ambiguous words if one reading qualifies.
  - Not allowed: any other inflected form, or the same word twice in one grid. Ingrid's dupe
    index blocks exact duplicates; variants like HAUS/HAEUSER are not caught and are left to the
    word check.
- No uniqueness check and no difficulty rating.

## 1. New binary `zkw` (Rust, `src/bin/zkw.rs`)

A separate binary on top of the library, so the existing `ingrid_core` CLI stays as it is. Cargo
picks up `src/bin/zkw.rs` automatically. `serde_json` becomes a normal dependency and output is
built with `serde_json::json!` (the crate's `serde` feature stays optional, no derives needed).

```
zkw generate --count 3 --start 1 --seed 1000 --out puzzles/
zkw refill puzzles/002 [--force]   # same pattern, next fill attempt (see 2.)
zkw pattern --seed 5 --count 50    # patterns + fill test, for the milestone-1 measurement
```

Shared options: `--wordlist wordlist.txt`, `--blocklist german-blocklist.txt`,
`--min-score 50`, `--max-len 10`, `--blocks 20..=24`,
`--fill-tries N`, `--max-patterns 30`.

### 1a. Reproducibility
- **No wall-clock limits in the decision path.** Whether a fill "succeeds within 5 s" depends on
  the machine, so the pattern loop would diverge between runs. The fill is capped by **number of
  tries** instead. A generous wall-clock timeout remains only as a safety net that aborts with an
  error, never as a reason to move on to another pattern.
- **Capped fill lives in the library.** `zkw` can't rebuild the retry loop itself: `Slot`'s
  fields are private, and `maintain_arc_consistency`/`calculate_slot_weights` are private too.
  So add `find_fill_capped(config, seed, max_tries)` to `backtracking_search.rs`, sharing the
  body of `find_fill_with_seed` (initial arc consistency, shared crossing weights, 500·1.1ⁿ
  backtracks per try) but returning `ExceededBacktrackLimit` after `max_tries`. Without it,
  `find_fill_with_seed` with no timeout loops forever on an unfillable grid.
- **Seeds:** every random stream gets its own seed from an explicitly chained splitmix64 over
  `(base_seed, puzzle_nr, stream_id, attempt)` — not std's `DefaultHasher`, whose output isn't
  stable across Rust versions. Stream ids for pattern, fill, hints and shuffle. Plain `seed + N`
  would collide with Ingrid's internal retries (`seed.wrapping_add(retry_num)`).
- No `HashMap` iteration order in any output: letter counts use a `[u32; 26]`, sorting is
  explicit. (Checked: the library only uses HashMaps for lookups, runs single-threaded, and the
  same seed gave identical fills across repeated runs.)
- `Cargo.lock` is committed for the `zkw` work (`SmallRng` output is not stable across rand
  versions). rand 0.10 API: `random_range`/`random_bool` (`RngExt`), `shuffle` (`SliceRandom`),
  `choose` (`IndexedRandom`), all via the prelude.
- Each `puzzle.json` records `pattern_attempt`, `fill_seed`, `refill_round`, the git commit of
  `german-blocklist.txt` used, and a hash of word list plus blocklist (to detect a mismatch). An
  old puzzle is regenerated by checking out that blocklist version.
- Known residual risk: Ingrid's slot ordering uses `log10` on floats, which could in principle
  differ across platforms. Accepted; puzzles are generated on one machine.

### 1b. Pattern generator (`src/pattern.rs`, in the library so it can be tested)
A review experiment showed that the first version of this design produced grids that almost
never filled: 8 of 8 patterns timed out at 10 s, while the original grid fills in 45 ms. The
search stopped at the first legal grid, which sat right at every limit. The generator is
therefore driven by a **quality score**, not just legality.

- **Representation:** `Vec<bool>` with 136 cells. Runs are computed the way Ingrid's template
  parser does it (1-letter runs are not entries).
- **Hard constraints** (must be 0):
  1. Every white cell belongs to at least one entry (2+ letters).
  2. No entry longer than `max_len` (10).
  3. All white cells connected (flood fill).
  4. No two blocks orthogonally adjacent (true of the original; configurable).
- **Soft targets** (weighted penalties, tuned in milestone 1), modelled on the original:
  - Entries of 9–10 letters allowed but penalized; at most 2 of them. The original's longest is
    8.
  - At most 4 two-letter entries (the original has 4; only 90 two-letter words score ≥50).
  - 40–52 entries in total (the original has 46).
  - Enough cells that belong to only one entry (the original has 17), since fully crossed areas
    are what make dense grids hard to fill.
- **Search:** simulated annealing over block moves, minimizing hard violations × large weight +
  soft penalties, run to a low target score instead of stopping at the first legal grid.
  (A prototype met all constraints and targets in under 500 steps; restart after 5k.)
- **Fill filter:** generate candidates and try each with `find_fill_capped`. The first that fills
  wins; its index is the `pattern_attempt`. At most `--max-patterns` (default 30) candidates per
  puzzle, then fail with an error.
- **Fallback if the success rate stays low** (kept in reserve): derive patterns from the
  original 17x8 by a few random block moves that keep the hard constraints.
- **Tests:** unit tests for each constraint, and a test that the original grid passes them all.

### 1c. Word list and blocklist loading
- Read `wordlist.txt`, drop every word in `german-blocklist.txt` (one word per line, `#`
  comments allowed, e.g. `BEKAM  # Präteritum`), and pass the rest to
  `WordList::new` via the existing `WordListSourceConfigProvider::Memory { words }`, with
  `max_length = Some(max_len)`.
- Ingrid normalizes words to lowercase: compare blocklist entries in lowercase, uppercase all
  output.
- **One-off pre-screen of short words.** Sample fills showed about 15% of entries failing the
  rules, concentrated in 2–3-letter junk (SH, DB, YIN, UE, AI, KZ). With a lazy check alone, a
  fully clean 46-entry fill is very unlikely. The ~450 entries of 2–3 letters at score ≥50 are
  checked once up front and the bad ones go on the blocklist; longer words stay lazy.
- The blocklist only removes words. Correct words with too-low scores (HAEUSER, 30) are never
  found this way; a `score-overrides.txt` can be added later if that limits the fills.

### 1d. Fill choice
- One fill per pattern for the pilot. Best-of-k by mean score is deferred: at score ≥50 and length
  ≤10 there are only two score values (50 and 55), so the mean barely distinguishes fills. If
  added later, rank by the number of words already in `german-approved.txt` instead.

### 1e. Hint selection
- **Hint word:** uniform among entries of 5 or more letters, across and down together.
- **Hint letter:** count letters over the grid's cells, each cell once, take the top 4 with ties
  broken alphabetically, pick one uniformly.
- **Revealed cells:** hint word cells plus every cell with the hint letter, as one set; its size
  goes into the JSON.

### 1f. JSON output (`puzzles/NNN/puzzle.json`)
```json
{
  "series": "Zuordnungs-Kreuzwort",
  "number": 1,
  "base_seed": 1000, "pattern_attempt": 3, "fill_seed": 8812301, "refill_round": 0,
  "blocklist_commit": "…", "wordlist_hash": "…", "words_checked": false, "solution_hash": "…",
  "width": 17, "height": 8,
  "pattern": ["......#.....#....", "..."],
  "solution": ["NICHTE#SPAET#GABE", "..."],
  "hint_word": {"word": "VEREINEN", "row": 7, "col": 1, "dir": "across"},
  "hint_letter": "E",
  "revealed": [[1,5], [1,9], "..."],
  "across": [ {"word": "ATOME", "row": 8, "col": 7, "len": 5, "score": 50}, ... ],
  "down":   [ {"word": "PRAG",  "row": 1, "col": 9, "len": 4, "score": 50}, ... ]
}
```
- `across` and `down` are **shuffled at random** (seeded); that order is final and is used as is
  by the clue doc and the PDF.
- Positions stay in the JSON for the solution page and checks; the puzzle page doesn't print
  them. Coordinates are 1-based, matching the existing `-w` output.
- `zkw` also writes `grid.txt` (the pattern) for `refill`, and on every run rebuilds
  `puzzles/index.json` from the puzzle directories, sorted (Typst cannot list directories).
  Rebuilding instead of appending avoids duplicates after refills or reruns.

## 2. Word check and refill (Claude, after each `generate`/`refill`)
1. Check every entry of each new puzzle against the allowed-forms rules. Words already accepted
   are cached in `german-approved.txt` and skipped. When all pass, set `words_checked: true`.
2. Add rejected words to `german-blocklist.txt` with the reason.
3. Run `zkw refill puzzles/NNN`. A full refill would bring new unchecked words every round
   (removing words changes the glyph counts Ingrid sorts by), so refill is **partial**: approved
   entries stay as pre-filled letters in the template, the cells of rejected entries are cleared,
   and only the reopened slots are filled. Only new entries need checking. Hints and shuffle are
   re-drawn afterwards.
4. If the partial refill can't be filled within the caps, fall back to a full refill of the same
   pattern; if that fails too, a new pattern under the same number (next `pattern_attempt`).
5. **Round cap:** after 6 rounds, stop and report to the user instead of looping.
6. `refill` refuses to run when `clues.json` exists or `words_checked` is true, unless
   `--force`. The clue doc is created only after the check passes, and read-back verifies the
   words against `puzzle.json`.
7. Report both the blocked words and the newly approved words, so the user can veto either.

## 3. Clue doc (Claude Docs, one doc per batch)
- Title "Zuordnungs-Kreuzwort – Hinweise Nr. 001–003", one section per puzzle, then
  *Waagerecht* and *Senkrecht* in the JSON order.
- Per word, three draft clues as checkboxes plus a line for an own clue:
  ```
  ATOME (5)
  ☐ Kleinste Bausteine der Materie
  ☐ Teilchen aus Kern und Hülle
  ☐ Gegenstand der Kernphysik
  ☐ Eigener Vorschlag: …
  ```
  The length is shown only in the doc, for editing; it never reaches the PDF. If the doc format
  doesn't support real checkboxes, fall back to "mark with ✓".
- The three drafts vary: a plain definition, a paraphrase, and a cryptic or associative clue.
  Clues must not contain the answer or a word sharing its stem. Aim for ≤ 40 characters so most
  clues fit on one line in the PDF.
- **Reading it back:** the ticked clue wins, unless the own line is filled in, which then takes
  priority. If nothing or more than one is ticked, flag the word and don't guess.
- Result: `puzzles/NNN/clues.json`,
  `{"across": [{"word": "ATOME", "clue": "…"}, …], "down": […]}`, in the same order as
  `puzzle.json`; the build checks that the words match.

## 4. PDF (Typst, A4 portrait)
- **Setup:** install the prebuilt Typst binary, version pinned in `scripts/setup.sh`.
- Always compile with `--root .` and project-absolute paths (`/puzzles/NNN/puzzle.json`);
  Typst cannot read files outside the root. The puzzle number comes in via `--input nr=NNN` and
  is read as `sys.inputs.nr`.
- **`typst/zkw.typ`**, template functions taking the parsed JSON, with cell size and font size
  as parameters:
  - **Puzzle page:** header "Zuordnungs-Kreuzwort Nr. 1"; grid at 10 mm per cell (170 × 80 mm),
    blocks solid black, revealed letters in bold sans-serif centered in their cells; below it
    *Waagerecht* and *Senkrecht* as two-column lists in JSON order, without numbers, positions
    or letter counts. Budget: ~46 clues ≈ 24 lines per direction pair at 10 pt ≈ 120 mm, so
    header + grid + clues ≈ 225 mm of the 267 mm text height. The font size is a fixed
    parameter, chosen after looking at the pilot pages.
  - **Solution page:** "Lösung Nr. 1" with the filled grid, no special marking of the hints.
- **Build files:**
  - `typst/einzeln.typ` → `puzzles/NNN/puzzle-NNN.pdf` (puzzle on page 1, solution on page 2).
  - `typst/sammlung.typ` → `sammlung.pdf`: reads `puzzles/index.json`, all puzzle pages in order,
    then all solutions at 8 mm cells, three to a page.
- **`scripts/build.sh`:** `typst compile --root . --input nr=NNN` per puzzle, then the
  collection.
- **Check:** render PNGs of the pages and look for layout problems before the user sees them.

## Milestones
1. **Pattern generator, measured first.** Implement `find_fill_capped` and 1b, generate 50
   patterns, and fill each once with its fill seed under the real try caps. Gate: at least ~1 in
   3 fill. Report the success rate and the median wall time per *successful puzzle*, including
   the time spent on failed candidates. Otherwise tune the soft targets or switch to the fallback.
   Show 5 sample patterns.
2. Pre-screen the short words; `zkw generate`/`refill` (partial refill) with blocklist and JSON;
   generate 001–003 and run the word check.
3. Typst template with placeholder clues; user reviews the layout.
4. Clue doc for 001–003; user ticks or edits, Claude reads it back.
5. Final PDFs (single files and collection); review and adjust before scaling up.
